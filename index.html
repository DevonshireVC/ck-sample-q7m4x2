Note 1: Collapse at the door (Dog A, seen Friday)

Collapse falls into three buckets: seizure, syncope, and falling over. A dog that stays aware fits falling over best. But an excitement trigger at the door points toward syncope. Arrhythmias come and go, so a normal heart on exam day does not rule them out. Why it matters: when the history includes an excitement or exertion trigger, it is worth keeping a cardiac cause on the list even when the exam is normal.

Note 2: Breaking the cough loop (Dog B, seen Friday)

In tracheal collapse, coughing irritates the airway, the airway collapses, inflammation and mucus build, and that drives more cough. Rozanski's first goal is to break the cough, reaching first for opioid antitussives (hydrocodone, butorphanol) given as needed and tapered, and saving prednisone mostly for dogs with lower airway disease. Why it matters: for an owner who isn't ready for prednisone, there is a well-supported place to start.

Note 3: The Onsior day limit and tablet count (Cat C, seen Friday)

The catch: One day over, and half the tablet dose each day.

The label limit is 3 days total, injection and tablets combined, one per day. An injection Friday plus one tablet a day for 3 days runs Friday through Monday: 4 days against a 3-day limit. The tablet dose also steps up at 13.3 lb: cats from 13.3 to 26.4 lb get 2 tablets once daily. At 16 lb, Cat C's chart called for 2 tablets once daily, but Cat C went home with 1 a day. Why it matters: after an injection day, at most 2 tablet days remain, at the tablet count for the cat's weight.
